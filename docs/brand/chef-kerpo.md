## TL;DR — the elevator pitch

Kerpo Gabriel de León is a Uruguayan-born former professional goalkeeper who spent his football career in Uruguay and Honduras (Danubio, Atenas, Municipal Valencia, Hispano, Platense, Vida, and Motagua), retired in 2013, and built *Lo de Kerpo* in Comayagua to bring his country's grill tradition — the *asado uruguayo* — to Honduras with a *toque catracho* (a Honduran touch). He's a self-described homebody who calls gastronomy an art, prepared for life after football for years before he hung up his gloves, and turned cooking-for-the-people-who-visited-his-house into a restaurant. The restaurant has been a Comayagua institution for over a decade.

The brand story is **"from the field to the grill"** — a hospitality-driven second act, not a celebrity-chef vanity project. Warm, family-driven, Uruguayan craft brought home to a Honduran city he chose to stay in.

**Honest note for storytelling:** he reached multiple semifinals in the Liga Nacional de Honduras but **never won a championship final**. This is *part* of the story, not a footnote — the man chose Comayagua and the grill over chasing a title that never came. The story is one of contentment, not consolation.

**Significant moment:** On **1 September 2012**, playing for Motagua against Marathón, he took a throat trauma when Mitchel Brown's arm struck his throat during a contested aerial ball. He kept playing, but it is one of the marked episodes in his late career.

### The transition — football to grill

- He told *Diario Más* in 2014 that the restaurant **was not improvised** — he had **declared himself a merchant five years before retirement**, knowing what he would do after football.
- He still supports **Danubio** (his Uruguayan club).
- Despite playing for Motagua in Tegucigalpa during 2011–2013, he kept ties to Comayagua (his wife's hometown) and based the restaurant there.

### Cuisine — the Uruguayan grill tradition

The brand specifically positions itself in the **asado uruguayo** tradition — distinct from Argentine asado in subtle but real ways. When writing copy:

- **Wood/charcoal-fired** parrilla, not gas.
- **Tira de asado** (short ribs cut across the bone) is canonical to Uruguayan asado.
- **Chorizo**, **morcilla** (blood sausage), **mollejas** (sweetbreads), and **provoleta** (grilled provolone) are standard *entradas* before the main meat.
- **Chimichurri** is served, but Uruguayan asado leans more on the meat itself than on sauce.
- The "toque catracho" — Honduran touch — likely means: local cuts when sensible, Honduran sides (plátano, frijoles, queso seco, encurtidos), and adapting to local palates. *Needs client confirmation on which specific dishes are the Honduran additions.*

When writing menu copy, default to **describing the technique and origin** (e.g., "Tira de asado — cortes a la cruz sobre brasas de leña"), not adjectives ("delicioso," "increíble"). Uruguayan asado is craft-driven; the copy should feel like a craftsman talking.

## Voice & tone — implications for the site

Anchored in everything above:

1. **First-person plural, conversational.** "*Hacemos asado uruguayo en Comayagua desde [año].*" Not corporate marketing-speak; the way a chef-owner would write his own About page.
2. **Spanish, neutral Río de la Plata–influenced.** Kerpo is Uruguayan; the brand name itself is Uruguayan. Lean *light* into voseo only if the client is comfortable — otherwise keep neutral Latin American Spanish that reads naturally to a Honduran audience. "*Lo de Kerpo*" stays as-is; never translate the brand.
3. **Hospitality before glamour.** He's *hogareño*. The site should feel like an invitation to someone's home, not a luxury reservation. Avoid words like *exquisito*, *gourmet*, *fine dining*. Prefer *casa*, *parrilla*, *mesa*, *sobremesa*.
4. **The story is "from the field to the grill."** This *Diario Más* headline (*"Kerpo de León, del campo a la parrilla"*) is the single best framing of his arc. Reuse this phrasing on `/historia` as a section header or pull-quote.
5. **Comayagua is home, not a footnote.** Kerpo chose to stay. Hispano made him an idol. Yeny is from there. The restaurant is rooted in the city, not parachuted in. Copy should reflect that — the Tegucigalpa branch is the *expansion*, Comayagua is the *origen*.
6. **Football is part of the story but not the headline.** He's a chef now. References to his playing days belong on `/historia` as the prelude, not on the home page. Resist the urge to lead with the "former goalkeeper" angle — let the food lead, let the man behind the food carry it.
7. **Family, not celebrity.** Yeny and Kendra are part of the story. The restaurant is family-owned, and the voice should honor that. *Needs client permission* before naming his wife or daughter publicly on the site — they may prefer to stay private.

### Words and phrases worth using verbatim (where the client confirms)

- *"Asado uruguayo con un toque catracho"* — the canonical tagline. Use unmodified.
- *"Del campo a la parrilla"* — the *Diario Más* headline; great as a section title on `/historia`.
- *"Lo de Kerpo"* — never translate, never abbreviate, never anglicize.
- *"Hogareño"* — if writing about him personally, this is his own word.

### Words and phrases to avoid

- "Gourmet," "exquisito," "fine dining," "premium dining experience" — wrong register entirely.
- "Steakhouse" — accurate but Anglo; prefer *parrilla* or *asado*.
- "Inmigrante," "extranjero" — he's naturalized Honduran and considers himself local. Frame him as *uruguayo-hondureño* or simply *de Comayagua*, never as a foreigner.
- Any framing that treats his football career as a failed first act (he chose the grill on purpose — see his five-years-of-merchant-prep above).
